Phase-Space Maps#

Config: configs/phase_space.yaml · Data: get_phase_space_map() · Module: uvex_transients.phase_space

How well UVEX detects a transient of a given peak luminosity and decline time, whatever its physics. A canonical transient is simulated over a map of the two, in the FUV and the NUV, and the region of the map each shipped transient class occupies is shaded on it.

Back to the report

The idea#

The Standard Run asks how many events of each class UVEX detects, which depends on the model of every class. A phase-space map asks a more general question that holds for any transient, including ones that are not modeled here: given an event’s peak luminosity in a band and its half-decline time (the rest-frame time from the peak until it has faded to half), what is the chance that UVEX detects it?

Every point of a map is a canonical transient, with a fast-rise, exponential-decay light curve and a power-law spectrum, whose two free quantities are the axes of the map. At each point, events are drawn at random positions and times of the survey, volume-weighted in redshift out to the distance at which the source falls below 25 AB, and observed against the same real UVEX schedule as the Standard Run. The expensive points are placed by a Gaussian process on the recovery fraction, which puts each new batch where one more simulation teaches the most about the map.

What is a detection?#

The map of a band counts an event as detected if at least two observations reach a signal-to-noise ratio of 5 in that band. As in the Standard Run, an event whose only qualifying observation is the first-ever visit to its field is not counted, since there is no reference image to detect it against. Because the band both detects the event and defines the luminosity, the FUV and NUV maps are separate.

Reading a map#

Each figure has four panels, over the half-decline time (horizontal) and the peak luminosity in the band, with the equivalent absolute AB magnitude on the right:

  • the recovery fraction \(f\), the fraction of the events drawn on the footprint that are detected,

  • the all-sky efficiency \(\eta\), the probability that an event exploding at a random position and time during the survey, within the redshift limit, is detected. Unlike \(f\) it does not depend on how the survey is divided in time,

  • the rate at which one detection is expected, relative to the local core-collapse supernova rate. The lower the number, the rarer a transient the survey can find there,

  • the uncertainty that remains on the fitted map, which is what the sampling spends its points to reduce.

The shaded regions are the shipped transient classes, each the smallest region that holds 68% of draws from its SED priors, measured in the band of the map. They show where a class sits, not how many of it UVEX detects: two events with the same peak and half-decline time can differ in rise, tail and colour evolution, which the map holds fixed. The yield of a class is its row of the Standard Run.

Reading the data#

The maps are made by uvex-transients phase-space configs/phase_space.yaml and attached to each release as one phase_space.zip. get_phase_space_map() fetches the files of one map, and load() rebuilds the model from points.ecsv to evaluate a map anywhere, at any resolution.
