Transient Yield Report#

The UVEX Transient Yield Report (TYR) is the expected transient science of the UVEX survey: how many events of each type UVEX should detect, given the latest published release of the survey schedule, the transient SED models and their volumetric rates. Every number on these pages comes from the data products of that release, and the pages are regenerated when the documentation is rebuilt.

Important

The TYR is a first look at the simulated data, not a final analysis. The numbers will change as the survey schedule, the transient models and the rates are refined. Detailed analyses, such as follow-up planning, classification or science-case-specific selections, are up to the user and the UVEX team, starting from the released data products.

Note

This page is generated from the latest published release, v0.4.0alpha, specifically its event_summary.ecsv data product. Every data product (the event catalog, photometry, exposure, and per-event summary) is attached to that release on GitHub, and earlier releases are listed on the releases page.

  • Yield Statistics for how the expected detections and their uncertainties are computed.

  • Transients for the SED model, rate and priors of every transient type.

  • get_results() for downloading a release’s data products.

Available Simulations#

The TYR contains several simulations, each with its own transient populations, selection and data products. The default is the Standard Run, which covers every registered transient class at full scale; the others each target one science case.

Transient Detection Report#

The 0th-order result of the TYR is the number of transients UVEX detects over its 2-year prime mission. Here, an event is detected if the survey observes its position after it explodes and, in at least one observation and in at least one band (FUV or NUV), its signal-to-noise ratio exceeds 5. An event whose only such observation is the first-ever visit to that field is not counted, since there is no reference image to detect it against. A detection says only that UVEX sees the event: it does not mean the event can be characterized or classified, which needs more observations and is a subject for downstream analysis.

The table gives, for each transient type of the Standard Run, the expected number of real events that UVEX detects. The first uncertainty is the 90% Clopper-Pearson interval from the finite Monte Carlo sample, and the second is the uncertainty on the event rate. The overlap columns are the percentage of those detected events whose sky position lies inside the footprint of ZTF (Palomar-visible sky) or Rubin (the LSST main survey and deep drilling fields). They are sky overlap only, and do not account for whether those surveys observed the event at the right time.

Simulations#

Each simulation is its own run of the pipeline, built for one science case: its own transient classes, parameter priors, sampling and selection cuts, and therefore its own data products. Each card below opens a gallery of the analyses done on that simulation. The examples download its data from the latest release with get_results() (cached after the first download), and the galleries are rebuilt with the docs, so they follow the latest release.
