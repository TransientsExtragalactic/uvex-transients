TypeIaSED#

class uvex_transients.models.supernovae.Ia.TypeIaSED(**overrides: Parameter | Quantity | float | int)[source]#

Type Ia supernova SED, attenuated by host-galaxy dust, with priors tuned to the ASAS-SN luminosity function.

Reuses LineBlanketedArnettDecaySED (radioactive-decay Arnett diffusion, floored photospheric temperature and a line-blanketed blackbody), with its parameters’ priors replaced and the host dust of HostDustMixin added. The priors follow Sarin et al.[1] (Sarin et al. 2026, a sample of 2205 SNe Ia from ZTF), except for M_Ni: M_ej is the population-level Gaussian from that paper’s hierarchical Arnett-model fit (\(\mu_\mathrm{ej}=1.26\,M_\odot\), \(\sigma_\mathrm{ej}=0.33\,M_\odot\)), kappa_gamma is fixed at the value adopted by Scalzo et al.[2] (Scalzo et al. 2014), and kappa is uniform over that paper’s marginalization range.

Tuning to the ASAS-SN luminosity function

The paper’s M_Ni Gaussian (\(\mu_\mathrm{Ni}=0.64\,M_\odot\), \(\sigma_\mathrm{Ni}=0.42\,M_\odot\)) gives a distribution of peak V-band absolute magnitude that is about 0.3 mag too bright, and too broad, against the ASAS-SN luminosity function of SNe Ia, even with host dust. The M_Ni prior is therefore a truncated Gaussian with \(\mu_\mathrm{Ni}=0.48\,M_\odot\) and \(\sigma_\mathrm{Ni}=0.2\,M_\odot\), chosen together with the mean of the \(A_V\) prior below by trial and error (compared by the total variation distance of the binned distributions), not by a fit. The peak V-band magnitude constrains only the combination of the two: other pairs fit equally well, so neither value is measured here. The distribution is still too thin at the faintest end (\(M_V\gtrsim-17.5\)), where subluminous events (91bg-like) are missing from a single Gaussian.

UV line blanketing

break_frequency (\(0.9\times10^{15}\) Hz, about 3330 Angstrom) and beta (5.5) were set against the peak \(\nu L_\nu\) of the Swift/UVOT SNe Ia (UVW1, UVM2 and UVW2), with the priors above. The first calibration (\(0.75\times10^{15}\) Hz, 5) predates the host dust and the tuned M_Ni, and left the model about 0.35 dex too faint in UVW1 and UVW2. The median peaks in both bands and the UVW1 to UVW2 colour now agree with the data (to 0.02 dex), but the UVW1 to UVM2 colour does not: the model is too bright in UVM2 by about 0.35 dex, and no choice of the two parameters fixes that. The Swift SNe Ia are a brighter and narrower sample than the ASAS-SN-like population of the model, so the agreement in level is only as good as that comparison, the colours are the more robust of the two, and the observed UVOT bands do not reach the UVEX FUV band, which is an extrapolation. Because the blanketing returns the flux it removes to longer wavelengths, changing it moves the V band too, so M_Ni was tuned again after it.

Host dust

The SED is reddened by dust in the host galaxy, with the G23 law (DUST_LAW) and a V-band attenuation \(A_V\) drawn from an exponential distribution with mean 0.4 mag. \(R_V\) is normal with \(2.70 \pm 0.25\), truncated to G23’s range of 2.3 to 5.6; the width is that of the uncertainty on the mean, which is within the 95% upper limit of 0.61 on the scatter of the population. The two are independent. The dust is part of the model, not a correction applied on top: nothing is known of the dust that shaped any given event, so the SED describes the luminosity we observe and infer, and eval_bolometric is the integral of the attenuated \(L_\nu\). A_V=0 recovers the dust-free SED. Milky Way foreground dust is separate (the ebv argument of the photometry methods).
