TypeIIPSED#

class uvex_transients.models.supernovae.IIp.TypeIIPSED(**overrides: Parameter | Quantity | float | int)[source]#

Type IIP supernova SED, with an optional early excess from confined circumstellar material (CSM).

A semi-analytic model of the spectral luminosity \(L_\nu(t,\nu\mid\theta)\) of Type IIP supernovae and of the IIP events whose early light curve is boosted by a confined shell of CSM, like SN 2023ixf and SN 2024ggi. Everything is closed form: there is no ODE integration and no grid beyond one small table of a normalization. Redshift, distance and dust are not part of it.

The form follows the temperature and bolometric evolution of SNe II, with a recombination floor [1], the colour temperature of shock cooling and the departures from a blackbody that suppress the UV [2][3], and the interaction power that emerges in the UV and shortens the optical rise of events with CSM [4][5]. The prevalence and brightness of the CSM events are set from the ZTF samples [6][7], and their parameters from the fits to SN 2023ixf and SN 2024ggi (Hiramatsu et al.[8], Shrestha et al.[9]), as the Notes describe.

Bolometric luminosity. The sum of an envelope, a radioactive tail and a CSM pulse,

\[L_\mathrm{bol}(t) = L_\mathrm{env}(t) + L_\mathrm{Ni}(t) + L_\mathrm{csm}(t),\]

with the plateau cutoff \(F(t)=1/\{1+\exp[(t-t_p)/w]\}\). The envelope is the shock-cooling excess and the plateau,

\[L_\mathrm{env}(t) = L_{50}\,10^{-0.4\,s\,(t-50)/100}\,\bigl[1+a_e e^{-t/\tau_e}\bigr] \bigl[1-e^{-(t/t_r)^2}\bigr]F(t),\]

with \(t\) in days, so that \(L_{50}\) is the observed plateau luminosity at day 50. The tail is gated to emerge only after the plateau, which is what keeps that true,

\[L_\mathrm{Ni}(t) = M_\mathrm{Ni}\bigl[(\epsilon_\mathrm{Ni}-\epsilon_\mathrm{Co})e^{-t/\tau_\mathrm{Ni}} + \epsilon_\mathrm{Co}e^{-t/\tau_\mathrm{Co}}\bigr]\bigl[1-e^{-(T_0/t)^2}\bigr]\bigl[1-F(t)\bigr],\]

and the CSM pulse, with \(x=t/t_c\), is \(L_c\,x^k e^{k(1-x)}\) up to its peak at \(t_c\) and \(L_c\exp[1-\sqrt{1+((t-t_c)/\tau_d)^2}]\) after it. It equals \(L_c\) at \(t_c\), with zero slope on both sides, rises as \(t^k\) and decays exponentially with the time \(\tau_d\). \(L_c=0\) is a plain IIP.

Colour temperature. An expanding photosphere with a recombination floor. Temperature is derived, never specified:

\[R(t) = R_0 + v_\mathrm{ph}t,\quad T_\mathrm{exp} = \left[\frac{L_\mathrm{bol}}{4\pi\sigma R^2}\right]^{1/4},\quad T_\mathrm{col} = \bigl[(f_cT_\mathrm{exp})^n + T_\mathrm{rec}^n\bigr]^{1/n}.\]

\(R_0\) is the progenitor radius of a plain IIP and the breakout radius when there is CSM.

Spectrum. A blackbody at \(T_\mathrm{col}\), suppressed in the UV by line blanketing and renormalized to \(L_\mathrm{bol}\),

\[\begin{split}L_\nu = L_\mathrm{bol}\,\frac{\pi B_\nu(T_\mathrm{col})\,S(\lambda,T_\mathrm{col})}{\sigma T_\mathrm{col}^4\, N(T_\mathrm{col})},\qquad S=\begin{cases}(\lambda/\lambda_c)^{\beta(T)}&\lambda<\lambda_c\\1&\lambda\ge\lambda_c\end{cases},\qquad \beta(T)=\frac{\beta_\mathrm{max}}{1+e^{(T-T_b)/\Delta T}},\end{split}\]

where \(N(T)=\int\pi B_\nu S\,d\nu/\sigma T^4\) is the fraction of the blackbody left after the suppression. The spectral shape therefore integrates to 1 at every time: the flux removed from the UV is moved to longer wavelengths, not lost, so that \(L_\mathrm{bol}\) is the luminosity actually emitted. The blanketing is off above \(\sim10^4\) K, where \(N=1\), so the early, hot photosphere is a plain blackbody and the renormalization only matters once the photosphere has cooled (\(N=0.94\) at 6000 K). \(N(T)\) is tabulated once for each set of the four UV parameters, on 400 temperatures from \(10^{3.3}\) to \(10^{5.7}\) K and 1500 wavelengths from \(10^{1.5}\) to \(10^{6.5}\) Angstrom, and interpolated linearly in \(\ln T\).

Population

The parameters are drawn from a hierarchical population, as in the specification: an event is a CSM event with probability \(f_\mathrm{csm}=0.36\) (the parameter csm), then \(L_{50}\) (and, for a CSM event, \(L_c\)) is drawn, then every other parameter given them. This is not the independent draw of the other models, so _sample_latent_parameters() is overridden, and the rules are these.

  • Luminosity trends. The priors of s, M_Ni and v_ph are those of an event with \(\log_{10}L_{50}=42\), and of t_c of one with \(\log_{10}L_c=43.15\). The mean of each, in the variable the parameter is sampled in, moves by a slope times the distance of the event’s luminosity from that reference (LUMINOSITY_TRENDS), with its truncation bounds unchanged. The plateau decline steepens and the nickel mass and the velocity rise with \(L_{50}\), and a brighter CSM pulse peaks later. A prior that is not a normal or a truncated normal is drawn as it is, without the trend.

  • Event class. t_r and R0 have one prior for a plain event, the parameter’s own, and another for a CSM event (\(\log_{10}(t_r/\mathrm{d})\) normal about \(\log_{10}1.2\) with width 0.40 and bounds \([-0.7, 0.9]\); \(R_0=1.4\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{cm}\times10^{N(0,0.20)}\) capped at \(3\times10^{14}\) cm), which is not a parameter and is changed in a subclass only. An event without CSM has L_c = 0, and the unused t_c, k and tau_d are set to 3 d, 2 and 3 d.

  • Fixing a parameter gives that value to every event, and the trends of the others follow it. csm=1 or csm=0 selects the class, and fixing L_c makes every event a CSM event unless csm is fixed to 0 (L_c itself cannot be fixed to 0, because it is sampled in \(\log_{10}\)).

  • Replacing a parameter with another Parameter keeps the trend if its prior is a (truncated) normal in the same variable as the one it replaces.

The result is a hierarchical population whose V-band peak luminosity function follows the ASAS-SN one, with the CSM share of a magnitude-limited sample near the observed \(\sim65\%\) (see the Notes).

Notes

The priors carry these tags: [LF] tuned to the ASAS-SN luminosity function of SNe II, [fit43] from fits of the plain model to 43 IIP bolometric light curves, [csm2] from fits to SN 2023ixf and SN 2024ggi, [lit] set from the literature and [assumed] not constrained by the data.

  • csm: 0.36 [lit], Bruch et al.[6] (more than 36%) and Hinds et al.[7] (\(36^{+5}_{-7}\%\)).

  • L50: \(\log_{10}L_{50}\sim N(41.26, 0.51)\) truncated to \([40.8, 43.3]\) [LF]. The faint cutoff is the faintest object of the 43.

  • L_c: \(\log_{10}L_c\sim N(43.00, 0.40)\) truncated to \([41.0, 44.8]\) [lit], chosen so that the CSM share of a magnitude-limited sample approaches that of Hinds et al.[7]. The luminosity function does not constrain it.

  • t_p \(N(105, 20)\) d truncated to \([60, 150]\); s \(N(0.8+0.5(\log_{10}L_{50}-42), 0.5)\) truncated to \([0, 3]\); a_e \(4.0\times10^{N(0, 0.20)}\), bounded in \(\log_{10}\) to \([-1, 1]\); tau_e \(10\,\mathrm{d}\times10^{N(0, 0.18)}\), \(\log_{10}\) to \([0.5, 1.5]\); M_Ni \(\log_{10}\sim N(-1.45+(\log_{10}L_{50}-42), 0.17)\) truncated to \([-3.3, -0.5]\), all [fit43].

  • v_ph: \(8\times10^8\,\mathrm{cm/s}\times10^{0.33(\log_{10}L_{50}-42)+N(0, 0.08)}\) [assumed], from \(L\propto v^3\). T_rec: \(N(6000, 500)\) K truncated to \([4500, 7000]\) [csm2], with the observed temperatures.

  • Plain events: t_r \(1.0\,\mathrm{d}\times10^{N(0, 0.35)}\), \(\log_{10}\) bounded to \([-1, 0.7]\) [fit43]; R0 \(3.5\times10^{13}\,\mathrm{cm}\times10^{N(0, 0.15)}\) [assumed], a red supergiant.

  • CSM events: t_c \(\log_{10}\sim N(\log_{10}3.5+0.5(\log_{10}L_c-43.15), 0.15)\) truncated to \([0, 1]\); tau_d \(1.5\,\mathrm{d}\times10^{N(0, 0.30)}\), \(\log_{10}\) to \([-0.5, 1.0]\), both [csm2]; k \(4.0\times10^{N(0, 0.30)}\), \(\log_{10}\) to \([0.18, 1.18]\) [assumed], with SN 2023ixf and SN 2024ggi (\(k\ge12\)) the steep tail; R0 and t_r as above [csm2], [assumed] (5 to 8 d for those two objects, the slow tail).

The model was checked against:

  • the bolometric curves of 43 IIP (plain model: a median 0.02 dex each) and the bolometric curve and temperature of SN 2023ixf and SN 2024ggi (0.05 to 0.07 dex in \(L\), 0.03 to 0.05 dex in \(T\)), with no UV data used (see below for those fits);

  • Swift/UVOT light curves (SOUSA, Brown et al. 2014): at the object level, SN 2012aw, SN 2008in, SN 2024ggi and SN 2023ixf (183 detections) scatter 0.20 dex about a +0.02 dex median offset, and at the population level (53 SNe II) the median offsets are below 0.2 dex in all three bands from day 0.6 to 15;

  • the ASAS-SN V-band peak luminosity function of SNe II, to 0.06 to 0.10 dex rms over the eight bins from \(M_V=-15.4\) to \(-20.0\) after fitting one overall normalization (about \(5.3\times10^{-5}\) SNe/Mpc3/yr). The brightest bin (\(M_V=-21.2\)) is not reproduced and was excluded;

  • a CSM share of a magnitude-limited sample (weights \(\propto10^{-0.6M_V}\)) of about 60%, against \(67\pm6\%\) from Hinds et al.[7].

The reference fits of the two CSM objects, which are curve-reproducing sets, not measurements (the early-excess and CSM terms trade off), are in the model’s tests.

Limitations

  • The late-time UV of the population is probably faint, by a factor of 2 to 4 after day 15. Selection effects in the Swift sample contribute (the data are 0.3 to 0.65 dex above the model median from day 15 to 110), but persistent interaction emission is not modeled. A one-parameter additive UV term was designed and deliberately left out.

  • The far UV (below 1900 Angstrom) is uncalibrated, since the blanketing was set on Swift bands only. There is no cut at the hydrogen Lyman limit.

  • The first day is an extrapolation: no plain IIP has a temperature before day 4 in the calibration set.

  • \(t=0\) is the explosion, not first light. With CSM, the model can be faint for a short time before an observer would record first light.

  • About 17% of the population peaks fainter than the faintest bin of the luminosity function (\(M_V=-15.4\)), and is unconstrained.

  • 37 of the 43 light curves come from a flash-spectroscopy sample, so part of the “plain” early excess is CSM.

  • After about day 80 the temperature of real events can fall below the floor (nebular phase), and the tail spectrum is a floor-temperature blackbody. A constant photospheric velocity makes the model cool slightly too fast over days 20 to 40 in some objects.

  • There is no host extinction, and the luminosity function was taken at face value.

  • v_ph, R0 of the plain events, k and the t_r of the CSM events are assumptions, not fits.
